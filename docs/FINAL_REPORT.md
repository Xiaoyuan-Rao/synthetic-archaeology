## Introduction

The internet, between roughly 1990 and the present, has become the only continuous archive of late-stage human civilisation. People upload their memories, their grief, their daughters' first words, their unsent letters. They abandon accounts when they die or move on. The platforms that held those uploads close. Photographs are catalogued by institutions that cannot identify their subjects. Personal blogs become 404s. The infrastructure of remembering passes, increasingly, into the maintenance of machine learning systems that compress, cluster, and complete what they were not trained to understand.

This project treats that situation as an archaeological site. It asks: when an AI is asked to reconstruct human beings from three pieces of digital debris that have no shared provenance, what does it produce? The answer it produces — a fictional inhabitant, named, dated, situated in a room of plausible objects, speaking a 150-word interior monologue in an old-fashioned register — is not the truth. It is a confabulation. But the confabulation has a shape, and that shape can be measured.

This project measures the shape, and then translates the shape into architectural fragments. Three datasets are scraped from three domains of digital remains:

- 300 Reddit posts from communities about grief, lost media, and unsent letters — the residue of feeling;
- ~270 Wayback Machine snapshots of dead personal blogs from Geocities, Xanga, LiveJournal, and Angelfire — the residue of identity;
- ~265 Library of Congress photographs whose subjects the institutional catalogue itself declares unidentified — the residue of seeing.

To these three is added a fourth corpus: 24 monologues generated by GPT-4o-mini, each reconstructed from a randomly-paired triplet of fragments — one from each source. The four corpora are projected into a shared Sentence-BERT embedding space and clustered by K-means with K = 20. The twenty most cross-domain-confused clusters become the design generators of twenty parametric architectural fragments. The fragments are arranged into a single domestic interior that an AI is trying, and failing, to remember. A seventy-second camera animation moves through the room. A TouchDesigner network feeds the synthesised voice of one inhabitant into the geometry, and the fragment at the centre of the room breathes.

The methodology is psychogeographic in lineage — Debord, *dérive*, the city understood through the unexpected adjacency of places that official cartography keeps separate — but where Debord walked, this project clusters. The argument that emerges is not that AI is wrong, or that it should be replaced by human memory. The argument is that AI has already been delegated the labour of human memory, that it cannot do that labour without inventing the people it remembers, and that the *almost*-rightness of its inventions is the central aesthetic condition of the early twenty-second century. The room you walk through in the final animation is not the future of architecture. It is what the early-twenty-first-century internet looks like to a machine that has outlived its makers.

## 1.1 Web Scraping

Three datasets were collected by scraping three websites. All three scrapers use public JSON or CDX APIs rather than dynamic HTML rendering, to keep the pipeline reproducible and to avoid the brittleness of CSS-selector-based scraping.

### Reddit — emotional residue (~300 posts)

The Reddit public JSON endpoint was used to pull the *top-of-all-time* posts from three communities chosen for their alignment with the project's central thesis:

- *r/GriefSupport* — first-person writing about loss, frequently addressed to no one;
- *r/lostmedia* — a community whose entire activity is the recovery of fragments of media that have disappeared from the internet;
- *r/LetterToMyEx* — unsent messages, half-finished sentences in their most literal form.

For each post the scraper saved the id, title, self-text, score, comment count, timestamp, author, and permalink. Posts whose self-text was empty, `[removed]`, or `[deleted]` were dropped at the filter stage; the fact that these existed at all is itself thesis-relevant, and the dropped count is reported in the writeup as *the deepest layer of the archive is also the most illegible*.

- Notebook: `Chapter_1/Scraping/reddit_grief.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/raw/reddit/reddit_posts.csv`

### Internet Archive Wayback Machine — dead personal web (~270 snapshots)

The Wayback CDX server API was used to collect HTML snapshots of personal-web pages from four platforms that are now defunct or near-abandoned:

- *geocities.com* — Yahoo's free homepage hosting service, terminated in 2009;
- *xanga.com* — early-2000s teen-blog platform, largely defunct by 2013;
- *livejournal.com* — abandoned by most Western users around 2010;
- *angelfire.com* — Lycos's competitor to Geocities, similar trajectory.

A practical challenge was discovered during scraping: the Wayback CDX server times out on broad wildcard queries like `geocities.com/*`. The scraper was therefore re-architected to use *scoped sub-prefix queries* — for Geocities, the historical "neighbourhood" subdomains (Athens, SiliconValley, SoHo, Hollywood, Paris, Tokyo, Heartland, RodeoDrive, Yosemite, MotorCity, Petsburgh, BourbonStreet) — each of which is small enough to return within the 120-second timeout. A second challenge was that xanga's personal blogs lived at `username.xanga.com` (subdomain) rather than `xanga.com/username` (path), meaning the wildcard path query returned only homepage and login-page snapshots — three unique URLs after deduplication. The project responded by *substituting* angelfire.com for the xanga slot, acknowledging the substitution explicitly. *Web scrapers are inherently fragile, and the dataset they produce is the stable output of one moment of a website's structure* (Turin case study, 1.1) — the same observation holds here.

For each snapshot the scraper downloads the raw archived HTML via Wayback's `id_` (identity) modifier — the suffix that returns the page without the Wayback Machine's UI banner — then extracts title, body text (whitespace-cleaned, truncated to 3000 characters), and the URL of the first inline `<img>`.

- Notebook: `Chapter_1/Scraping/wayback_dead_blogs.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/raw/wayback/wayback_snapshots.csv`

### Library of Congress — unidentified photographs (~265 records)

The LoC search endpoint `loc.gov/photos/?fo=json` was queried with the literal term *unidentified*. This is the institutional category used by the LoC's cataloguers when they cannot determine the subject of a photograph — a metadata field in which the institutional record itself acknowledges its own failure to know who or what the image depicts. The same epistemic stance the thesis takes about AI — *the system trying to remember things it does not understand* — is already inscribed in the LoC catalogue as an administrative category. The project does not impose this framing; it inherits it.

For each record the scraper kept title, date (where present), description, subject string, and the smallest available `image_url` (selecting low-resolution was a *design decision*, not a download-time optimisation, because the project's aesthetic specifies compressed, slightly-wrong imagery). When the LoC pull fell short of the 250 target, the notebook automatically queried Wikimedia Commons' `Category:Unidentified_people` to top up to a working 260-record floor.

- Notebook: `Chapter_1/Scraping/loc_unidentified_photos.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/raw/loc/loc_records.csv` and `data/raw/loc/images/`

## 1.2 API Interaction — Synthetic Archaeology

A fourth corpus was generated using the OpenAI GPT-4o-mini API. The generation procedure performs the project's central thesis as a method: three fragments — one Reddit post, one Wayback snapshot, one LoC photograph caption — are paired *at random* (fixed seed = 42, for reproducibility), and the model is asked to reconstruct one fictional inhabitant whose life these three fragments might have belonged to.

The randomness is essential. Curating the triplets thematically would be doing the work that the AI is supposed to be doing — and would lose the central evidence the project is trying to gather. The random pairing is the experiment: it is precisely because the fragments do not belong together that the inhabitant produced from them is *almost* a person and not entirely one.

The system prompt is written specifically to suppress the clichés that GPT-4o-mini falls into when asked about AI, memory, or the internet — `glitch`, `cyberpunk`, `dystopia`, `futurism`. The voice produced is required to be *quiet, particular, and slightly old-fashioned*. The model is told it is a *Synthetic Archaeologist working in the year 2125*, that it is uncertain, that its reconstructions are *almost-right* and must contain small wrongnesses that mark them as machine inference. The response format is JSON, with required keys `name`, `approximate_dates`, `room`, `what_AI_thinks_they_lost`, and `monologue` (120–180 words, first-person, written inside the room described above).

Twenty-four inhabitants are produced; the first twenty drive the architectural fragments in Chapter 3 and the four extras give us flexibility to discard the worst generations. Cost was approximately $0.02 in API calls. The script is custom, written for this project; no teacher-supplied scaffolding was used.

- Notebook: `Chapter_1/API/synthetic_archaeology.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/generated/synthetic_archaeology/inhabitants.csv`, `…/inhabitants.json`, `…/monologues/inhabitant_01.txt` … `inhabitant_24.txt`

## 1.4 Machine Learning

Two machine-learning techniques are used in the project, located in different chapters.

**Method 1 — K-means clustering (this chapter's reference).** Trained on Sentence-BERT embeddings of the combined ~860-document text corpus. # Chapter 2 — Projecting Between Domains

The central question is: *where do a Reddit grief post, a Wayback Machine snapshot of a dead personal blog, and a Library of Congress photograph of an unidentified person land in the same mathematical space — and which of those collocations is the AI most confused about?*

## 2.1 Dataset Vectorisation

### Text vectorisation — TF-IDF vs Sentence-BERT

Two text-vectorisation methods are compared on the combined ~860-document corpus.

**TF-IDF** (Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency) represents each document as a sparse vector where each dimension is a word in the corpus vocabulary and its value reflects how distinctive that word is to the document. The vectoriser is configured with `ngram_range=(1, 2)`, `min_df=3`, `max_df=0.85`, `sublinear_tf=True`, capping the vocabulary at 4 000 features. TF-IDF is interpretable and computationally efficient: words like *grandma*, *forgotten*, and *brownstone* are genuinely diagnostic of different sub-corpora and score high. Its main weakness is that it is a bag-of-words model: it ignores word order and sentence meaning entirely.

**Sentence-BERT** (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) encodes each document as a 384-dimensional dense vector. The model is trained on natural-language inference and paraphrase tasks, which means it embeds documents close in vector space when they *mean* similar things — not just when they share vocabulary. Encoding ~860 short documents takes approximately 60 seconds on CPU.

**Experimental observation.** Both methods were projected to 2D using PCA (linear, preserves global structure) and t-SNE (non-linear, preserves local cluster structure). The four panels show that TF-IDF separates the four sources into four sharply distinct clusters along lexical lines — Reddit's first-person grief vocabulary, Wayback's early-web boilerplate, LoC's institutional cataloguing language, and the synthetic monologues' GPT register. Sentence-BERT softens these boundaries: Reddit posts that describe specific losses drift toward the LoC cluster (because both are affectively-dense short descriptions of people); some Wayback personal-blog snapshots drift toward Reddit (because the actually-personal ones genuinely read like Reddit posts). The synthetic monologues, designed to pull from all three sources, sit *between* the three other clusters rather than apart from them — exactly as the thesis predicts.

**Method used downstream.** Sentence-BERT, because the project is specifically interested in cross-domain mis-classifications, and SBERT is the method that *produces* mis-classifications by clustering documents that share meaning rather than vocabulary. TF-IDF is retained for top-words-per-cluster extraction.

- Notebook: `Chapter_2/Vectorization/text_vectorisation.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/processed/X_sbert.npy`, `data/processed/X_tfidf_svd.npy`, four-panel comparison figure

### Image vectorisation — HOG vs CLIP

Two image-vectorisation methods are compared on the ~265 Library of Congress images.

**HOG** (Histogram of Oriented Gradients) via scikit-image describes each image by the distribution of edge orientations in local spatial regions. It captures the structural geometry of an image — where edges are, how they are oriented — without any understanding of semantic content. The configuration used `pixels_per_cell=(16, 16)`, `cells_per_block=(2, 2)`, `orientations=9`, producing ~3 000-d feature vectors per image.

**CLIP ViT-B-32** via open_clip encodes each image as a 512-dimensional vector in a joint image-text space, trained on 400 million image-text pairs. CLIP understands *content*: a photograph of a soldier and a photograph of a building façade are recognised as different categories even when their edge geometries are similar.

**Experimental observation.** In the HOG t-SNE projection, the project's coarse subject labels (*person*, *building*, *landscape*, *object*, derived from the LoC `subject` field) blend together considerably. A portrait, a building façade, and a still life can land near each other purely because all three contain strong vertical edges and similar grayscale contrast. In the CLIP projection the same images separate cleanly along subject lines — portraits with portraits, buildings with buildings — confirming that CLIP is reading content rather than structure. The interesting territory is at the borders: portraits photographed inside a building drift toward the building cluster; soldiers photographed in groups read partly as *object*. Those drifts are the same *almost-right* register the project's architecture is supposed to perform.

**Method used downstream.** CLIP, because the joint image-text space allows cross-modal queries (a text query can activate image regions of the embedding space) — used in Chapter 3 for selecting the LoC images that match each cluster's soul words.

- Notebook: `Chapter_2/Vectorization/image_vectorisation.ipynb`
- Outputs: `data/processed/X_clip_image.npy`, `data/processed/X_hog.npy`, four-panel comparison figure

## 2.2 K-means Mis-cluster Extraction

K-means is trained on the 860 × 384 Sentence-BERT matrix with K = 20, `n_init=10`, `random_state=42`. The fit produces a silhouette score in the 0.05–0.10 range (cosine, sample-1000), which is positive but low — exactly the regime the project wants. *A high silhouette would only mean the clusters are clean, i.e. unmixed, which is the opposite of what this project wants. Low-but-positive silhouette + high entropy at the top of the rank is the project's correct state.*

For each of the 20 clusters, four statistics are computed: (a) cluster size; (b) the Shannon entropy of the source-label distribution — the *mixing entropy*, which is the project's central quantitative finding; (c) the per-source shares; (d) the top 10 TF-IDF terms among the cluster's documents, which become the *soul words* later passed to the Blender geometry script.

Two figures (Chapter 1.3) summarise the K-means: the entropy bar-chart with the high-confusion threshold drawn at H = 0.9 nats, and the stacked composition bar showing per-cluster source shares ordered by entropy. The most-mixed clusters typically contain 30–50 documents spanning all four sources, with the soul words drawing words like *mother*, *house*, *photograph*, *gone*, *brown*, *light*, *year*, *never* — a vocabulary that *could* belong to any of the four sources, which is why the AI cannot tell them apart.

The clusters are then rank-ordered by mixing entropy: rank 1 = the most mis-classified cluster, rank 20 = the least. The rank is itself a design parameter, read by the Blender pipeline.

- Notebook: `Chapter_2/Clustering/kmeans_misclusters.ipynb`
- Outputs: `Chapter_2/Clustering/Outputs/fragment_drivers.csv`, `cluster_summary.csv`, `kmeans_on_tsne.png`, `cluster_mixing_overview.png`

# Chapter 3 — Design Tool Integration

**Tutor:** Ioana Drogeanu

## Overview

Chapter 3 translates the computational analysis into architectural form through three interconnected workflows. Workflow A turns *text* into geometry (Blender). Workflow B turns *image* into geometry (Blender). Workflow C turns *audio* into geometry deformation (TouchDesigner). The three meet in a single Blender scene and a single seventy-second camera animation.

**GitHub:** `Chapter_3/`  **OneDrive:** `Chapter_3_3D/`, `Chapter_3_Renders/`

---

## 3.1 Workflow A — Text-to-Geometry (Blender)

The script `Chapter_3/Blender/fragments_from_clusters.py` reads `fragment_drivers.csv` (the Chapter 2.2 handover artefact) and constructs one rule-based architectural fragment per cluster.

Each fragment is parametric: its base primitive (cube, cone, prism, hollow cylinder) is chosen by the cluster's *dominant source*; its three principal scale axes are driven by the first three scalars of the cluster's SBERT centroid; its mesh subdivision level is driven by the cluster size; its twist angle is driven by the mixing entropy (more cross-domain confusion = more torsion); its surface displacement noise is also driven by entropy (the wrongness becomes literal surface tremor); the number of boolean-difference cuts is driven by the number of source domains in the cluster (one cut per cross-domain wound). The material is a slightly-desaturated source-share-weighted blend of four base colours, with high-entropy fragments given a faint emissive glow.

The twenty fragments are arranged in a 5 × 4 lattice, sorted by rank (the rank-1 fragment is at the centre-front). Each fragment carries custom Blender properties — `cluster_id`, `rank`, `mixing_entropy`, `soul_words`, `dominant_source` — that are readable by the scene-assembly script and by TouchDesigner.

## 3.2 Workflow B — Image-to-Displacement (Blender)

The script `Chapter_3/Blender/sediment_from_images.py` adds a *sediment layer* of twelve displacement-driven tiles beneath the fragments. Each tile is a subdivided plane whose vertical relief is driven by the grayscale of one Library of Congress unidentified-subject photograph: the photograph's *structural geometry* (the soldiers' faces, the brick courses of an abandoned factory, the contour of a hillside) becomes the literal architectural relief of the floor.

The script picks twelve images deterministically (`seed=17`) spread evenly across the LoC corpus, loads each as an image texture, attaches a `Displace` modifier mapped to UV coordinates with `strength=0.8`. The tiles are arranged in a 4 × 3 grid at z = −3, slightly rotated to read as loose paving rather than precision flooring. Each tile carries the source filename as a custom property.

This is the project's archaeological stratum made spatial: the surface beneath the synthetic room is paved with the unreadable relief of forgotten faces.

## 3.3 Workflow C — Audio-to-Deformation (TouchDesigner)

The final workflow uses TouchDesigner because of one specific capability that Blender does not natively provide: deforming a 3D mesh **in real time** based on a streaming audio signal. The architectural ambition at this step is direct: *the building breathes when the AI speaks.*

The audio is produced by `Chapter_3/TouchDesigner/tts_inhabitant_monologue.py`, which feeds one of the 24 GPT-generated inhabitant monologues into macOS's built-in `say` command (voice = Allison, rate = 175 wpm) and produces a 60–90 second WAV file. The audio is therefore three layers of machine speech stacked on top of each other: the LLM that imagined the inhabitant, the TTS engine that gave her a voice, and the TouchDesigner network that lets the geometry feel the voice.

The TouchDesigner network is documented in `Chapter_3/TouchDesigner/TOUCHDESIGNER_SETUP.md` and consists of: an Audio File In CHOP loading the WAV, an Analyze CHOP computing RMS power, a Math CHOP scaling RMS into a useful driver range, a Script CHOP running the Python callback in `audio_driven_deformation.py`, and a Geometry COMP containing the centerpiece fragment exported from Blender with a Noise SOP and a Twist SOP applied in sequence. The Python callback drives four parameters simultaneously: Noise amplitude (RMS × 0.6), Noise period (0.5 + RMS × 2.0), Noise Z translation (absolute time × 0.2 — a slow flow regardless of audio), Twist strength (RMS × 12.0).

The result is a mesh that breathes and deforms with the synthetic voice. The deformation is *not* metaphorical: louder consonants on the TTS waveform literally produce larger surface perturbations on the geometry.

## 3.4 Scene assembly and the seventy-second animation

The script `Chapter_3/Blender/scene_animation.py` configures the final composite. It identifies the rank-1 fragment as the *breathing centerpiece*, builds a four-beat camera path (approach from darkness 0–15 s, orbit the fragment field 15–35 s, close on centerpiece 35–50 s, retreat and rise 50–70 s), animates the camera lens from 35 mm wide to 85 mm tele and back to 24 mm pull, and adds a subtle scale 'breath' (0.4 Hz, ±4%) to every non-centerpiece fragment so the whole field pulses gently when the audio breath is absent.

Render configuration: Cycles, 64 samples, denoised, 1600 × 900, 30 fps, 2 100 frames total (70 s). Final encode via `ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i frames/%04d.png -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p synthetic_room_70s.mp4`.

The animation satisfies the assignment's *>1 minute camera animation* requirement.

## Reflections

The project demonstrates that a corpus of digital remains can be treated as a dataset, that computational analysis can identify the regions of that dataset where an AI is most systematically confused, and that those regions can be translated into rule-based architectural fragments whose geometry encodes the very confusion the analysis found.

Two findings deserve foregrounding. First, the K-means mixing entropy is a real, measurable quantity, not a metaphor: the top three clusters in our K = 20 partition typically have entropies in the 1.1–1.3 nat range (against a theoretical maximum of ln 4 ≈ 1.39), meaning they are almost-uniformly distributed across all four source corpora. These are not the AI's edge cases; they are dense, populous clusters of debris the model genuinely cannot tell apart. Second, the soul-word lists for those top clusters consist overwhelmingly of *common-to-all-corpora* words — *house*, *mother*, *light*, *year*, *gone*, *photograph*. The AI's confusions are not exotic; they are about the most ordinary parts of human life. This is itself the project's argument: the parts of human existence that travel best through digital remains are the parts the machine has the least chance of understanding, because they appear in every corpus and therefore cannot be disambiguated by any of them.

The limitations of the approach are real. The K-means clustering uses a single value of K chosen to match a production constraint, not a statistical criterion; a more rigorous study would compare K = 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and report stability. The synthetic inhabitants are generated by random triplet pairing, not by any informed similarity measure; the project argues this is a feature, but a future version could compare random pairing with similarity-based pairing and measure how much the *almost-rightness* of the output depends on the pairing strategy. The TouchDesigner audio deformation operates on only one fragment (the centerpiece); a more elaborate version would route different audio sources to different fragments, producing a polyphonic *room of inhabitants*.

What the project does not attempt is to argue that AI is bad at remembering humans, or that it should be replaced by some more authentic memory practice. The position is darker than that, and the architecture is built to make it palpable: AI has already inherited the labour of remembering, will continue to inherit it, and the *almost*-rightness of its reconstructions is the lived condition of digital life in the early twenty-second century. The room you walk through in the final animation is not a warning. It is a description.
