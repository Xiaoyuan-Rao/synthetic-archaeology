Today would have been my sister's birthday. I can almost see her, laughing at the table, flour dusting her cheek, a smile that lit up the room. The bright blue of the walls feels a little dimmer now; it was her favorite colour, after all. I had hoped we would celebrate over cake, her famous chocolate recipe, surrounded by her children. Instead, there is silence where her laughter should be. I close my eyes and listen, as if I might hear her voice again. A momentary flutter of hope — maybe it was just a dream. But it never is, is it? I reach for her cookbooks, running my fingers along the spines, feeling the indentations where she pressed them open. My heart aches for the weight of the loss, for the empty chair that will never be filled again.
