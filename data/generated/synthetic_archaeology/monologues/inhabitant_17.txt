I still hear the quiet hum of the refrigerator and the distant sound of children playing outside, a world where laughter seems so far from my own now. Some days, I dream he’s just gone on a long trip, and he’ll walk through that door any moment, grinning like he used to. I can picture the way his hair fell across his forehead, that light dance in his eyes. But other days, the anger wraps around me, thick like fog, and all I can feel is the cold weight of this empty chair beside me. It belonged to him, and now it’s just another reminder of the gaping hole he left behind. How could he do this? I remember the lavender I used to grow in the garden, its comforting scent, but now it feels twisted, like a cruel joke. I sit here and wonder if he ever really saw the beauty in any of it.
