# Margaret Halloway (1972-2021)

Room: Margaret's living room is small and filled with worn, overstuffed furniture. The fabric is a faded floral pattern, soft from years of use. A thick, woolen blanket, speckled with colors of autumn leaves, is draped over the arm of the couch. On the coffee table, a half-empty cup of chamomile tea rests beside a stack of family photographs, their edges curling and yellowing with age.

What AI thinks they lost: The companionship and presence of her daughter.

---

The light filters through the sheer curtains, casting a soft glow on the walls, as if it were trying to warm the chill that lingers here. I can still smell the cinnamon from the candle I lit last week, its scent one of the few things that has not faded entirely. I remember her laughter, bright and carefree, echoing in these corners. They say time heals, but I find it fills the space with echoes rather than solace. Each day I glance at her empty chair at the dining table, the porcelain plate still waiting for her favorite pasta. I can almost hear her voice, asking if she can have more cheese, that delightful, teasing way she had of asking. It is a strange kind of silence that grows louder in her absence, a reminder that life continues to move, while I remain here, caught in the stillness of what once was.
