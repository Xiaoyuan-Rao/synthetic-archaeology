# Evelyn Parker (1992-2023)

Room: Evelyn's room was modestly decorated, with soft, beige walls and a worn, floral armchair near the window. In one corner stood a small bookshelf crammed with dog-eared novels and travel guides, while the scent of lavender from a small sachet hung in the air, mingling with the faint smell of old paper. A colorful quilt, handmade by her mother, lay draped over the bed, its patches vibrant against the muted tones of the room.

What AI thinks they lost: Her brother, who understood her in ways no one else could.

---

I sit in this chair, the same one I always made my brother sit in when he visited. The fabric is soft, though it has a few frayed edges now, like the memories I have of him. I catch glimpses of the afternoon sun spilling through the window, casting golden patches on the dusty floor. The lavender sachet still gives a gentle scent, reminding me of our afternoons spent in the garden. I can almost hear his laughter, echoing through the house during those warm summer days. He would have loved the way the light dances along the quilt that covers my bed, how it brightens the colors and makes everything feel alive. I wish he were here to tell me about the things that captured his imagination, the stories he spun with his words. How strange and precious life felt when he was around; now it seems a little dimmer without him.
